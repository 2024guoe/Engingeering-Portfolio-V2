Rocketry

New Collins Research Rocket

Systems lead for a team rocketry project; defined research areas, technical specs, budget constraints, and led subteams to engineer a 30k ft apogee rocket with experimental airbrakes.

Why

A large component of scoring in the International Rocket Engineering Challenge (IREC) involves launching a rocket that flies to a target apogee. My team experimented with airbrakes as a calculated method to hit the exact altitude. We also carried a research payload looking at the effects of zero-gravity crystallization

How

  • Organized subteams for each rocket component such as Avionics, Structures, Recovery, and Airbrakes. Managed a 14-person team along with the technical project.
  • Held weekly check-ins and guided each subteam through design, simulation, and build challenges; hosted a design review with professors, graduate students, IREC staff, and SSI alumni to gather thorough feedback
  • Organized 4 test launches throughout the year, scaling up in rocket size and design requirements to test designs and validate simulations
  • Experimented with new simulation softwares that allowed for greater rocket complexity; eventually creating one unique to the team rocket

Result

We verified working airbrakes during launch, although not as successful or consistent as would be necessary for IREC. However, during the process, I developed better aerodynamic models around unique rocket shapes, wind shear, and pressure differences at altitude. I also grew as a leader and team manager.

Mechanics

Flappy Bird Automaton

Flapping bird automoton with pre-determined Kestrel-like wing motion driven by calculated CAMs.

Why

Wanted to learn different manufacturing techniques in the Stanford Product Realization Lab (PRL) in order to better understand fabrication processes and DFM.

How

  • Extrapolated a Kestrel flapping wing motion and designed CAM shapes to achieve that motion
  • Performed hand calcs and FEA to analyze friction between CAM and follow shaft and find balance between friction reduction and DFM
  • Designed and CADed casting molds/blanks for aluminum sandcasting process
  • Fabricated and finished project using the lathe, mill, wood CNC, forge, waterjet, sandblaster, laser cutter, bandsaw, grinding wheel, and buffing wheel

Result

Created a working automaton and presented to PRL CAs and professors during quarterly university-wide Meet the Makers makerfaire.

Rocketry

Hybrid Rocket Injector

Researched, developed, and modeled an injector which atomizes and mixes high velocity, temperature, and pressure fluids for more efficient combustion

Why

Hybrid rockets like the one my team was working on require injectors to better spray fluid from the fluid fuel tank into the solid combustion chamber. The injector is the interface between the two types of fuel in the rocket engine.

How

  • Conducted thorough research into hybrid rocket injector methods and high pressure/velocity mixing
  • CADed several different injector types based on literature
  • Coded MATLAB script to model different injector types' diffusivity and velocity profiles of fluid particles
  • Ran FEA on injector with ANSYS to ensure its survival and safety factor in high temperature and pressure environment
  • Narrowed down injector type, toleranced it. Team project unfortunately went unfinished due to unrealistic project scoping, but a downsized version of my injector design was used in the 2025-2026 team rocket

Result

Although the team project I designed my injector had to be ended, my design was successfully used in a next-generation rocket cold flow. This was also my first big project as a Freshman in college, and I was able to learn so much about R&D and engineering from it.
