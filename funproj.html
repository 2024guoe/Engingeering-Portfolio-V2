Resin Art

River Epoxy Serving Platter

Epoxy resin running like a river through a walnut board with riverbed topography CNC'd in

Why

I wanted to make a functional and beautiful apartment working gift for my older sister!

How

  • Started off by CADing the general shape of the Chicago river (near where she is living). I originally wanted to import some riverbed topography from the USGS, but then I realized the Chicago river is man-made and has no interesting river bed topography. I ended up just CADing my own topography.
  • Created a CAM file from the CAD for Stanford PRL's shop bot and bought all the supplies I needed to make the CAD into a real gift--walnut wood, food-safe resin, food-safe resin dyes, etc.
  • Used the CNC to make the topography I needed and experimented with different color combinations for the epoxy and dye to make the perfect river!
  • Sanded everything down to 3000 grit, then used butcher block oil to create a perfect finish.

Result

My sister loved the gift, and it now sits as a center piece on her apartment table!

CAD/CAM Wood CNC Epoxy

Welding

Welded floating cube

Oxy-Acetylene Welded a floating cube out of steel

Why

The floating cube was used in the Stanford PRL's Cube Ceremony, which takes place once a quarter and celebrates all things cube.

How

  • Started off with two pieces of scrap sheet steel that poked corner holes into, cut, and bent with various sheet metal tools.
  • Used the oxy-acetylene torch to make 12 perfect welds around the edges of the cube!

Result

My welds were airtight and I did not need filler material--both things not being true would have caused the cube to sink

Sheet Metal Bending Oxy-Acetylene Welding

Hammer

Hammer

Casted, turned, milled, CNC'd, and finished a hammer

Why

The hammer was part of a hammer and tool-box project to learn how to use all of the Stanford PRL's machinery

How

  • Started by turning and tapping the two plastic portions at the ends of the hammer out of two different hardnesses of plastic, and then turned and tapped the handle out of scrap aluminum.
  • Sandcasted the hammer head out of bronze, and sanded it for hours to 800 grit, then buffed and used the CNC to inscribe a funny moniker into the head.
  • Taped off half the hammer and sandblasted the other half to create an interesting finish before melting in some heat set inserts and assembling!

Result

Works well as a hammer, and it is great decor!
